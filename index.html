St. Augustine and The Nights of Lights

St. Augustine

Founded by Spanish Admiral Pedro Menéndez de Avilés in 1565, St. Augustine is the oldest continuously occupied European-established settlement in the continental United States. For centuries, it served as a strategic military outpost for the Spanish Crown, defended by the massive coquina stone fortress known as the Castillo de San Marcos. Today, the city’s narrow streets and colonial architecture remain a living testament to over 450 years of Spanish, British, and American history.

Nights of Lights

The Nights of Lights is a world-renowned holiday festival that illuminates St. Augustine's historic district with over three million white lights. The tradition is inspired by the Spanish custom of lighting candles in windows during the Christmas season to signify hospitality and "making room" for travelers. This glowing display spans roughly 20 blocks and has been named one of the top holiday light displays in the world by National Geographic.
